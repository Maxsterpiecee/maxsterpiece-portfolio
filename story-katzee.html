Katze

A lone Stray navigates the ruins of a collapsed world, chasing a hum that no one else dares to follow. What she finds in the city’s forbidden heart changes everything.

Deep in the heart of the city, a strange hum creep through the concrete, traveling through the bones of bygone buildings. Steel skeletons – like a dying choir – cry out to a deaf audience; the survivors of the Collapse. The people ignore it, persisting in their own, dead worlds, one dreary day at a time. For years, none of the survivors dared to search for the source of the hum. But today, Katze was going to be the first.

Katze extended her hand to the haggard husk before her. “Have you seen this man?”

The wilted woman stared at the withered piece of paper – a photo of a father and daughter. “I…” One side of her face was gilded by the empyrean light of a setting sun. Its ethereal touch caressed her creased skin, “I’m sorry dear – I don’t know.”

Standing in the centre of the settlement, Katze’s eyes stayed on the faded piece of paper, but her mind drifted to the city beyond. The photograph was the only memory she kept of him; the only memory of the days before the Collapse. Katze’s wavering voice was more of a murmur, “It’s okay.” She sunk her teeth into her lips, inviting the flourish of pain; wanting it to overwhelm and wash away the pain in her heart. “It’s okay,” she repeated, but as her eyes moved away from the photograph, Katze realised the woman was gone.

Beneath a starless sky, Katze rested on the chassis of her motorbike, Nightingale – a machinist’s masterpiece, crafted from odds and ends she scavenged from her ventures to the city. Her pale fingers pawed at the photograph of her and her father. For the last couple of years, Katze had been a courier between the settlements – Sanctuaries as they were called. With Nightingale, she was the only one suited to make the runs – and the only one brave enough to do so. And as a Stray, Katze knew most of the city’s streets; she had spent countless days and nights on them, struggling, suffering, but learning.

“I-” the seams of her voice became frayed, “I don’t know what to do.” The whisper lingered for a moment, hanging from the dead air. Before pocketing the photo, Katze shut out the world and pressed it to her lips. Like a cat’s paw, the coarse cracks in the paper softly scratched her skin. “I love y-” but then, the words were quelled by the bellows of a falling building.

Before collapsing, the structure struck one of the neighbouring skyscrapers, and a shower of scrap rained upon the already ruined city. The collision casted out a deafening wail. For most of the survivors, the sound was a reminder of the world they’d lost in the Collapse. But for Katze, it was a chance to find new pieces of the past; a chance to build a new world from the old one and continue her father’s dream – the dream to save what was left of the world. With a glimmer in her eyes, Katze mounted Nightingale. After a series of switches, the machine purred, stirring to life. She had to make it to the fallen building with whatever daylight she had left.

As Katze continued down deserted streets, dodging debris, Nightingale’s purr became a howl. No one who lived in the Sanctuaries dared to go this deep, but Katze was a Stray; she had no sanctuary – no home to hide herself in. But even as a Stray, she knew the shadows of the streets were becoming darker, the roads, less and less familiar. Katze had mapped most of the metropolis, but this territory was the only exception. She had never been to this part of the city, but she knew what it was; the beginning of the Dead Zone. All of the rumours she had heard drift between the settlements were now before her, manifested by metal. Sliding to a standstill, Katze pounced from Nightingale. She’d made it to the base of the building – a cement cemetery. The air was strangely stifling, and Katze sensed it. Her ears perked up, and for the first time, she heard felt it… the hum. Katze poised herself at the resonance that followed. Then, one by one, the artificial lights of the city flared to life. She stood between the metal monuments, drowning in a pool of colour. Katze studied her hands – admiring the second-skin of light. But then, as quickly as it appeared, the glamour faded, and the hum was gone. Katze’s eyes stayed fixed on her hands as though the colour was still there. She had to tell the survivors; the old world was not dead – she had felt its heartbeat. Katze turned from the Dead Zone and bestrode Nightingale once more. As her bike howled to the nearest Sanctuary, she had missed the man further in the Dead Zone, standing… watching.

Katze arrived at one of the first Sanctuaries since the Collapse – the Barn. On foot, Katze made it to the front of a house; a chaotic collage of metal sheets and steel scraps. It was unwelcoming, but Katze continued to the porch, and invited herself in.

Seated at a makeshift hearth, an old man peered from the fire to Katze, who was standing by the door. It was a moment before he recognised the girl. “Oh, it’s you, Katze,” the man’s voice was shaky, but stubborn like the rest of his body, “you’ve not been here in years.”

Katze’s voice was cold, “I know, Ovince.”

“So,” the old man’s attention was back to the hearth, his movement and words drawn out like an ache, “what is it you want.”

“I…” Katze paused, thinking of the words to say – thinking of how to explain what she just witnessed. “I was in the city, Ovince. It’s not dead.”

Silence was the only answer Katze received.

“I know you don’t believe me.” You didn’t believe my father either… “But Ovince, you know the city’s history – you’re the only one!” Katze’s voice was climbing, “You were there during the-”

“Enough!” The fireplace hissed with a tongue of its own. “I am not doing this again, Katze. Get out.”

“What’s beneath the city, Ovince.”

The features of the old man’s face, flashed by flickers of firelight, revealed nothing.

“What is it-” Katze’s impatience was heating up, “the hum.”

Ovince’s eyes were on her at the sound of that word. “It’s nothing Katze…”

“I know it’s not just nothing.”

“It was the cause of the Collapse, Katze!” Ovince barked through gritted teeth. “I warned him not to go, but just like you, your father was obsessed by it. And now, you are too. If you go disturbing a dead world, you’ll end up the same as him.”

The words cut deeper than any of the other scars on Katze’s body.

“It’s dead, Katze. The world’s dead.”

No… none of it made sense. The hum; she’d heard it in the heart of the city. “I-I was in the city, Ovince. The city’s lights were on.”

“Impossible.”

“It happened!” Katze snapped at the old man. “You didn’t believe my father, and now, you don’t believe me.”

“Katze,” Ovince waved a hand to the door, “the survivors of the Collapse want nothing to do with the old world. And no one believes in a new one.”

“I do.” Katze touched the photo within her pocket. “My father did.”

A muscle flinched in the old man’s jaw.

“Whatever it was, it’s in the Dead Zone.” Katze started for the door, “I have to go find it.”

“Wait, girl.” Ovince nodded his head to one of the nearby shelves, “There.” A bundle of fabric was tucked between books and a lifeless pot plant. “And after this, I’m even – with you and your father.”

Katze snatched the fabric from the shelf, “What is it?”

“A piece of the old world,” Ovince watched Katze undo the folds, revealing a revolver, “the kind you Strays would never find out there.”

“Ovince, this is… How did you-”

“Go.”

Katze paused. Then, without word, walked out the door, stalking to the gates of the Barn, and readying herself for the Dead Zone, once more.

Returning to the heart of the city, Nightingale’s black coat tore through the streets. Katze had made it to the Dead Zone. She continued on foot, sticking to the shadows, searching for the entrance of the city’s underground metro. The people who resided here in the Dead Zone were Strays too, but Katze was the only one who helped the Sanctuaries. Unraveling the bundle of cloth, Katze eyed the memento for a moment. She weighed the loaded revolver in her hand, reminded herself of the rumours, then tucked the gun under her belt.

Within the metro, Katze plunged herself into a blackened world. With a free hand, she sifted through the satchels strapped by her waist. Then, several scratch-like sounds later, the tunnel was illuminated. The flare dyed the darkness before her a blood-red. The city’s heartbeat was close – she sensed it.

Clawing her way through the underbelly, Katze crept between ruins and rubble. Just as the flare had begun to falter, she made it to a room, and in the distance, a gentle glow. Katze freed the gun from her side and started forward, to the source of light. She made note of the room; signs of someone being here before. Then, Katze paused her prowl. She stood before the source of the hum; the cause of the Collapse. The Core. Dormant. Her eyes fell from the Core, finding a pair of glasses on the floor; broken.

No. Katze released her hold of the revolver. No, no, no…

With dilated eyes, she pulled the photograph from her pocket and studied it; her father’s glasses. They matched. Then, before she could sense the stranger’s presence, a voice whispered from behind.

“Katze?”

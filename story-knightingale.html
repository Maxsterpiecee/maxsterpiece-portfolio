Knightingale

Far from home and running out of time, a Knight and his Healer travel through cursed, unmapped lands in search of the Chalice — a relic that may not exist. Something in the dark watches them both. Chapter 1 of an ongoing work.

Work in Progress

Chapter One

Daybreak

“You’re losing your warmth.”

The embers of the fire crackled, whipping up to the open sky. The midnight air was rich with dew. I sat there, watching the fire bite at the dampness between us, again and again. It was a fight it would not win.

My armour – marred and muddied from the journey – weighed upon me, heavier than my own thoughts. Home was a many day ride from here now. We would be here for only a few more. At least I hoped. Seated by the fire, I shifted my weight in a different direction, trying to find a position where the armour would not bite into my skin. It was too much trouble to take off when we were stopped for short respites such as these. I looked to the man whom I travelled with; my companion, a man, younger than me, well versed in the practice of alchemy. For a while, I watched his hands. They were steady. He was grinding some herb he had scavenged from the forest floor earlier. Then, he added another; one he had cut from a fallen log, overgrown with moss. The walls of the stone bowl turned to a dark green.

He moved over, quickly, and with those steady hands, applied a layer of the wild salve to the gash at the side of my arm. He pressed it further into the wound. A deep, earthy scent stifled my senses, blocking out even the thick smoke. He did not see the way the black veins were already pulsing under the skin, mapping out my end. He did not want to see it.

My eyes were then upon the fire once more. It was a pathetic thing, clinging to the damp wood as desperately as it could. I stared until my eyes burned. Toward the edge of the light, I noticed it. The Feline was there, crouched on a fallen cedar. It was a hole in the night. A shadow with no source. It did not speak, but I heard it nonetheless: He’s patching a corpse, Knight. And you’re letting him.

I reached within my cloak, into my leathers, and fingered the locket that was within. A reminder. I closed my eyes, trying to remember her face. There was only the darkness.

The healer returned to his spot by the fire. He was swift, rolling the leather bundle of herbs closed, then securing it to his side. There were many other bundles that draped from his clothes: dark vials, dry rations, things that would make this journey a little lighter. He glanced at the surrounding thicket, past where the Feline was watching. He did not see it. There was a question there, written upon his face.

“Say it.”

He looked to me. “How are you sure it exists?” His eyes lowered to his hands as he fingered the wraps around the palms of his hands. “The Chalice, I mean.”

The Chalice. The reason we were here, far from our familiar beds and warm meals. A legend. No one knows where it originated, some say it was from a dying drunk, others from an age-old book in the castle’s library. It mattered not. It existed, and now here I was, in search of the very thing that could save us… and save her.

“It exists,” I said. The words felt like gravel in my throat. I believe in it, and yet, it was difficult to say so to this man across from me.

You tell him a lie, Knight, the shadow breathed.

“It has to exist,” I whispered, more to the locket than to the Healer. I reached for my sword; a lengthy, solid piece of steel. It had served me well for years, and would continue to do so. I eyed a rock by the fire, and took it into my palm. Its surface was smooth.

The Healer’s features remained still. Uncertainty.

I pressed the stone to the steel, and began to move it along the edge of my sword. The steel was aged, weathered with many scratches and marks, and its sheen was stained deep by blood.

“Get some sleep.” I wanted to ease his thoughts, but I did not know what else to say. He did not choose to come on this journey. We were sent against our own judgement. “We will leave at daybreak. We will make it to the village tomorrow.”

That night, I was kept from sleep.

My arm flinched at the crack of every branch, the rustle of every leaf. My sword was by my side, gilded by the golden bronze firelight. We were deep within the woods, parts that had yet to be mapped; far from the nearest settlement too, and even further from home. There was no one here, I told myself. Though, a part of me did not believe it. I had come to put my trust in this part of me; it had proven itself many times. I sat up from the grassy bed of midnight dew. The darkness was different here. It stirred as though living. The same darkness that I felt at my wound.

The healer was still asleep, nestled within his layers of rags and tattered cloth. I had not even asked his name during the many days we have been traveling together. Maybe it is for the best, then, I thought. There is no need to become closer. We departed as strangers, and we will return the same.

The sky was a little less dark once I awoke. Streaks of black clouds were scarring the sky; it would rain, there was no question about it. I only hoped it would not be much.

Our fire had now become a small mound of ash. Small, soft beads of light could still be seen between the darkness there. It was on its last breath. The healer was awake too, preparing the belongings thoughtfully, making sure nothing was misplaced. He treated this practice like it meant everything to him, I could tell. I do not judge him for it; I was the same at his age. I had kept my armour oiled and shined, my leathers washed, my sword sharp. I tried to continue the latter, but it has become more of a ritual now; an act of habit. The chipped and brittle edges could only take so more.

I pushed past the ache in my body as I rose, shaking the stiffness from my arms. There was no saying what we would find today; no one from the castle had been this far since the corruption had rooted itself into these lands. We could not take any survivors with us – if there were any. Our resources waned with each day, and we were spreading them as much as we could already.

“Are you ready?” My voice was still rough.

The Feline was watching from the treeline again. I held its gaze until the Healer finished packing, until the ash of our fire was cold and grey.

You don’t believe in it, it breathed. The daybreak between the branches seemed to pass through its figure.

I turned from it.

“No,” I said quietly, more to myself than the shadow. “But she does.”

I fastened my sword at my side and started through the trees without looking back. Behind me, I heard the Healer follow.

The Feline did not.
